The high-arched Charms classroom was bathed in warm afternoon sunlight, the air thick with the rustle of parchment and the gentle scent of beeswax candles. Perched atop a towering stack of thick leather-bound spellbooks behind his desk was the tiny Charms master, Professor Flitwick, his white mustache twitching with cheerful enthusiasm as he surveyed his first-year students.

"One of a wizard's most rudimentary skills is levitation, or the ability to make objects fly," Professor Flitwick announced, his squeaky voice echoing crisply across the stone room. "Uh, do you have your feathers?"

Hermione Granger instantly raised her pristine white feather into the air with a look of eager readiness. Right beside her, Ruhi smoothly held up her own feather, her calm, golden-brown eyes reflecting complete, centered composure.

"Good," Flitwick squeaked, nodding in approval. "Now, uh, don't forget the nice wrist movement we've been practicing. Hmm? The swish and flick. Everyone."

"The swish and flick," the entire class chorused, sweeping their wands through the air in unison.

"Good. Oh, and enunciate," Flitwick reminded them, raising his own wand. "*Wingardium Leviosa.* Off you go, then."

Immediately, the classroom filled with a chaotic chorus of first-years murmuring, chanting, and flicking their wands at their respective feathers.

Across the room, Draco Malfoy drew his wand back with dramatic flourish. "*Wingardium Levio-saaa!*" he drawled, his feather twitching uselessly on the desk.

"Wingardium Leviosa..." echoed around the tables as students struggled to get their feathers off the wood.

Sitting beside Hermione, Ron was growing visibly frustrated. His feather hadn't budged a single inch. Gritting his teeth, Ron began thrashing his wand violently through the air like a sword, shouting at the top of his lungs, "*Wingardrium Leviosar! Wingardrium Leviosar!*"

Hermione’s hand shot out, grabbing Ron's wrist to halt his wild movements. "No, stop, stop, stop! You're going to take someone's eye out. Besides, you're saying it wrong. It's Levi-*o*-sa, not Leviosar."

Ron glared at her, pulling his arm back, his face turning as red as his hair. "You do it, then, if you're so clever! Go on, go on!"

Hermione straightened her posture, pulled her shoulders back, and executed a flawless, crisp movement with her vine-wood wand. "*Wingardium Leviosa.*"

A faint blue luminescence bathed her feather as it smoothly detached from the desk, floating gracefully into the air until it hovered a full two feet above their heads.

Professor Flitwick clapped his hands in sheer delight. "Oh, well done! See here, everyone, Miss Granger's done it! Oh, splendid!"

Ron let out a miserable groan, slumping forward and burying his face directly into his open textbook in utter dejection.

A few tables away, Seamus Finnigan was leaning low over his desk, poking furiously at his feather with his wand tip. "*Wingard Leviosa... Wingar—*"

"Well done, dear!" Flitwick was still praising Hermione when—

*BOOM!*

An almighty, blinding flash of orange fire and black smoke erupted from Seamus's desk, shaking the nearest wooden benches. Flitwick gasped violently, tumbling slightly off his stack of books. "Whooaaa! Ooh!"

Ron and Hermione whipped their heads around toward the source of the blast. The smoke slowly cleared to reveal Seamus sitting frozen in his chair, his face completely charred pitch-black for the second time that week, his hair sticking up in spiky, smoldering tufts. On the desk, his feather had been reduced to a tiny pile of smoking gray ash.

Harry turned around in his seat, looking at the charred mess before looking up at the teacher. "I think we're going to need another feather over here, Professor."

While the rest of the class giggled and Flitwick rushed to fetch a fresh feather for Seamus, Ruhi turned her attention to her own desk.

She looked down at the soft white feather resting before her. While standard Western magic relied on forceful command—demanding the object to obey through vocal incantation and rigid wand-work—Ruhi’s training as an Ichhadhaari Naagin and a devotee of Lord Shiva taught her a far deeper cosmic truth: all matter carries a subtle prana, a sleeping spark of divine life.

Instead of forcefully brandishing her wand, Ruhi picked it up with liquid, serpentine grace. She didn't flick her wrist with sharp demand. Instead, she closed her eyes for a brief heartbeat, centering her breath, and softly chanted the incantation like a sacred mantra:

"*Wingardium Leviosa...*"

As the words passed her lips, Ruhi channeled a microscopic thread of her gentle, life-giving *prana-shakti* through her fingers, imbuing the feather not with force, but with living vitality.

The feather didn't just rise—it awakened.

It shivered softly on the desk, its delicate barbs glowing with a warm, golden radiance. Then, as if expressively thanking Ruhi for bestowing a moment of living consciousness upon it, the feather swirled upward in a breathtaking, spiraling ballet. It danced around Ruhi's head in a gentle, affectionate orbit, fluttering past her cheek like a pet bird before settling into a weightless hover directly above her palm.

Hermione’s mouth fell open as she watched the feather's extraordinary, organic movement. It wasn't just floating under the strain of a spell; it was dancing with joy.

"Ruhi..." Hermione whispered, utterly spellbound. "That's... that isn't standard levitation. It's moving as if it's alive!"

Harry watched Ruhi with wide, admiring eyes, a warm, proud smile breaking across his face. The soft golden aura reflecting in Ruhi's brown eyes made her look utterly ethereal, radiating a ancient grace that touched his heart.

Ruhi offered Hermione and Harry a soft, modest smile, gently lowering her hand as the feather glided down to rest silently on her palm.

"Magic is not a master commanding a slave, Hermione," Ruhi spoke softly, her voice carrying the depth of ancient wisdom. "When you offer life and respect to the object, it does not fly because it is forced—it flies because it wishes to honor you."

Professor Flitwick, having witnessed the golden spiral from his desk, wiped a tear of pure academic joy from his eye. "Breathtaking! Absolutely extraordinary, Miss Ruhi! Five points to Gryffindor for such profound magical harmony!"

Across the room, Draco Malfoy gritted his teeth, scowling in bitter jealousy as Harry and Ruhi exchanged another sweet, lingering look in the quiet afterglow of the spell.
