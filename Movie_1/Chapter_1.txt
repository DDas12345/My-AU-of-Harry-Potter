The golden bells of the ancient Shiva Mandir chimed softly as a gentle breeze swept through the stone corridors. Inside the inner sanctum, the air was thick with the fragrance of burning camphor, sandalwood, and fresh marigold flowers.

Three-year-old Ruhi sat cross-legged on a small tiger-skin mat right in front of the massive swayambhu Shiva Lingam. She was dressed in a beautiful, miniature golden lehenga that shimmered under the warm glow of the diyas. Her tiny hands were folded, her eyes tightly shut in deep concentration. Despite her tender age, an ethereal, divine aura surrounded her. Small, faint golden scales would occasionally glint along her collarbone before vanishing back into her soft skin—a clear sign of her royal lineage as the ansh-avatar of Sheshnaag.

"Om Namah Shivaay... Om Namah Shivaay..." her sweet, innocent voice echoed through the silent temple.

Sitting right beside her was Uttara Maa, the elder of Naaglok and Ruhi’s beloved Gurumaa. Dressed in a pristine white saree, her eyes reflecting centuries of wisdom and mystical power, Uttara Maa guided her softly.

"Apni saansokigati par dhyaan do, beta. Mahadev ke naam ko apne bheetar mehsoos karo," Uttara Maa instructed in a calm, soothing tone. ("Focus on the rhythm of your breath, child. Feel Mahadev's name within you.")

"Om Namah Shivaay..." Ruhi whispered, her mind perfectly aligned with the cosmic energy of Lord Shiva.

Suddenly, the tranquil atmosphere of the temple was shattered. A frantic, high-pitched screech tore through the air. A flash of crimson and gold blurred past the temple pillars. It was Agni, the mini-dragon. His small wings flapped furiously, and his tiny scales were covered in soot and dirt. He landed heavily on the stone floor right in front of Ruhi, panting heavily, his small reptilian eyes wide with sheer horror and grief.

Ruhi gasped, her meditation abruptly broken. She opened her large, expressive eyes. "Agni? Kya hua? Tum itne dare hue kyun ho?" ("Agni? What happened? Why are you so scared?")

Agni let out a sorrowful whine, his voice trembling with devastating news. "Choti Rajkumari... Mujhe... Mujhe Devi Nageshwari ka shav mil gaya hai. Woh... Woh ab is duniya mein nahi rahiin!" ("Little Princess... I... I have found the corpse of Devi Nageshwari. She... She is no longer in this world!")

The words struck like a thunderbolt. Ruhi’s tiny heart skipped a beat. "Nahi... Meri Maa... Meri Maa ko kuch nahi ho sakta!" she cried out, her voice cracking as tears instantly welled up in her eyes. ("No... My Mother... Nothing can happen to my Mother!")

Uttara Maa’s face turned deathly pale. Without wasting a single second, the wise elder closed her eyes and raised her hand, invoking her dibya drishti—the divine spiritual vision. Her eyelids flickered as her consciousness traveled across thousands of miles, crossing oceans and continents, searching for the residual aura of the future Naagrani’s mother.

Within moments, a horrifying vision flashed before Uttara Maa's inner eye. She saw a dark, damp, unfamiliar European forest, shrouded in thick fog. She saw the cold, brutal hand of a dark, snake-like wizard wielding forbidden black magic. She felt the horrific agony, the violation, and the ultimate sacrifice of the beautiful and powerful Nageshwari before her life force was cruelly snuffed out and her body buried like a secret in the dirt.

Uttara Maa opened her eyes, gasping for breath, her face filled with absolute rage and profound sorrow.

"Maa... Kahan hai meri Maa?" Ruhi sobbed, pulling at Uttara Maa’s saree. ("Maa... Where is my Mother?")

"Hamein turant jaana hoga, Ruhi," Uttara Maa said, her voice shaking with emotion as she gently wiped the little girl's tears. ("We must leave immediately, Ruhi.")

A few days later, a somber, heartbreaking atmosphere hung over a secluded, sacred riverbank.

A funeral pyre had been meticulously built out of sandalwood. Atop it lay the lifeless, pale body of Devi Nageshwari. Even in death, her face possessed a regal, divine beauty, though the hidden scars of the brutal assault she endured from the dark wizard Voldemort remained etched in her silence.

Three-year-old Ruhi stood before the pyre, holding a small burning wooden torch. Her tiny hands were shaking, not from fear, but from an overwhelming torrent of grief and newly ignited fury. Uttara Maa stood right behind her, placing a supportive hand on her small shoulder.

"Apni Maa ko mukti do, Ruhi. Unhe agni ke hawale karo," Uttara Maa whispered softly, tears rolling down her own wrinkled cheeks. ("Give your Mother liberation, Ruhi. Commend her to the fire.")

With a heavy heart, Ruhi touched the torch to the wood. The flames caught instantly, leaping up toward the sky, wrapping Nageshwari's body in a warm, consuming embrace. As the smoke rose into the heavens, something inside the three-year-old girl changed forever. The innocence of a toddler vanished, replaced by the fierce, unyielding spirit of a warrior queen.

Ruhi closed her small fists, her eyes suddenly flashing a brilliant, dangerous golden hue. Her scales rippled intensely under her skin. She looked at the rising flames and took a sacred, unbreakable oath.

"Main, Sheshnaag ki ansh, apni Swargwasi Maa ki chita ke samne yeh kasam khati hoon... Jis kisi ne bhi meri Maa ke saath yeh darindgi ki hai, jisne unhe mara hai... Main use chodungi nahi. Main us qaatil ko dhoond nikalungi," Ruhi declared, her voice carrying a chilling resonance that echoed across the river. ("I, the avatar of Sheshnaag, swear before my late Mother's funeral pyre... Whoever committed this monstrosity against my Mother, whoever killed her... I will not spare them. I will find that murderer.")

She turned her fierce gaze toward Uttara Maa. "Gurumaa, Agni ne kaha ki woh jagah kisi jadugari duniya ke paas thi. Mujhe wahan jaana hai. Mujhe us school mein jaana hai... Kya naam hai uska? Hogwarts! Mujhe Hogwarts jaana hai!" ("Gurumaa, Agni said that place was near some magical world. I have to go there. I have to go to that school... What is its name? Hogwarts! I have to go to Hogwarts!")

Uttara Maa looked at the young girl, deeply moved by her resolve. She knelt down to be at eye level with Ruhi.

"Tum wahan zaroor jaogi, beta. Main khud tumhara margdarshan karungi," Uttara Maa said firmly. "Lekin abhi tum bahut choti ho. Hogwarts mein dakhila lene ke liye tumhari umar kam se kam 11 saal honi chahiye. Tumhe us jadugari duniya ke taur-tareeqe seekhne honge, aur sabse zaroori... tumhe apni Naagin shaktiyon par poora niyantran paana hoga taaki koi tumhari asliyat na jaan sake." ("You will definitely go there, child. I myself will guide you. But right now you are too small. To get admission in Hogwarts, your age must be at least 11 years. You have to learn the ways of that wizarding world, and most importantly... you must gain full control over your Naagin powers so no one can know your true identity.")

Ruhi wiped her tears, her jaw set in determination. "Main intezar karungi, Gurumaa. Aur main har woh sabak seekhungi jo mujhe us qaatil tak pahunchayega." ("I will wait, Gurumaa. And I will learn every lesson that will take me to that murderer.")

For the next eight years, the Shiva Mandir and the hidden realms of Naaglok became Ruhi's ultimate training grounds.

Under Uttara Maa’s strict and loving guidance, Ruhi underwent rigorous physical, mental, and spiritual training. She learned how to harness the immense, cosmic energy of Sheshnaag flowing through her veins. She mastered the art of Ichchha-dhari transformation, learning to shift from her human form into her majestic, royal golden Naagin form seamlessly, and back again without leaving a single trace of magical residue.

Because she was a royal Ichchhaari Naagin, she possessed an innate, ancient mystical blessing: she was naturally multilingual. Languages came to her like breathing. While she and Uttara Maa always spoke in their native Hindi inside the temple, Ruhi effortlessly mastered English, Latin, and the various ancient dialects of the magical world, knowing she would need them to blend in perfectly at Hogwarts.

She trained until her reflexes were lightning-fast, her senses sharper than any apex predator, and her mind as calm as a silent ocean, hiding the raging storm of vengeance beneath.

Now, at eleven years old, Ruhi stood at the entrance of the Shiva Mandir. She was no longer the helpless toddler in a golden lehenga. She had grown into a stunning, poised, and fiercely intelligent young girl.

As expected, a strange brown owl flew across the Indian skies, dropping a thick parchment envelope sealed with purple wax right into her hands. It was addressed to her.

Ruhi looked down at the crest of Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry. A cold, sharp smile touched her lips. She looked back at Uttara Maa, who gave her a proud, reassuring nod.

The time of preparation was over. The future Sheshnaagrani was finally ready to step into the British wizarding world. When she was in India, she was the devoted daughter speaking in her mother tongue, but the moment she stepped onto British soil, she would speak their language, play their games, and dismantle the dark power that took her family away.
