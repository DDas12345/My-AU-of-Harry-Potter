The Gryffindor table was buzzing with excitement, every member beaming with pride and warmly welcoming Ruhi into their house. The older students clapped her on the back, enchanted by her serene grace, while Ruhi accepted their warm welcomes with a polite, regal head nod.

As the chatter filled the great hall, Professor McGonagall took a metal spoon and gently clinked it against her goblet. *Tink, tink, tink.*

"Your attention, please," McGonagall announced, her sharp voice instantly cutting through the noise.

At the center of the head table, Professor Dumbledore rose gracefully from his high-backed golden chair, extending his arms with a wide, welcoming smile. "Let the feast begin!"

Suddenly, an aerial view of the Great Hall revealed a magnificent sight. Golden platters, sparkling silver bowls, and massive serving dishes materialized out of thin air along every inch of the four long tables. They were piled high with roasted chicken, succulent sausages, mountains of mashed potatoes, gravy boats, steaming vegetables, and freshly baked breads.

Harry’s jaw dropped open. Ruhi’s eyes widened slightly in quiet amazement.

"Wow," Harry and Ruhi uttered together in perfect, effortless unison once again.

Across the hall, Draco Malfoy surveyed the massive spread of food on the Slytherin table, raised his eyebrows in satisfied approval, and immediately dug in. Beside Harry, Ron didn't waste a single second, grabbing chicken legs in both hands and stuffing his face with glorious enthusiasm.

A boy sitting nearby with sandy hair leaned across the table. "I'm half and half," Seamus Finnigan introduced himself between bites. "Me dad's a Muggle. Mam's a witch. Bit of a nasty shock for him when he found out."

Neville Longbottom let out a hearty laugh at Seamus's story. Ruhi offered a gentle, amused smile, leaning toward Harry, who was sitting right next to Percy Weasley, the Gryffindor prefect. Harry leaned over to Percy, gesturing toward the staff table.

"Say, Percy, who's that teacher talking to Professor Quirrell?" Harry asked.

Percy swallowed his food and glanced up at the high table. "Oh, that's Professor Snape, head of Slytherin house."

"What's he teach?" Harry pressed further.

"Potions," Percy answered cleanly. "But everyone knows it's the Dark Arts he fancies. He's been after Quirrell’s job for years."

Ruhi glanced subtly toward the head table. She caught Snape’s dark, unreadable eyes flicking toward her once again before he resumed his conversation with Quirrell. She knew Snape was part of the hidden loop regarding her mother, and she resolved to observe his movements carefully.

Meanwhile, Ron, having just polished off a chicken wing, reached into a massive silver bowl to grab another piece. Suddenly, a pearlescent, translucent silver ghost popped right out of the platter of turkey!

"Ahh!" Ron yelped, jumping backward and nearly dropping his plate.

"Hello! How are you?" the ghost greeted cheerfully, floating a few inches above the table. "Welcome to Gryffindor."

Before the first-years could fully process the sight, numerous ghosts came pouring right out of the surrounding stone walls, sailing gracefully through the air above the feast. A jolly, round ghost laughed merrily—"Whoo-hoo-hoo!"—while another ghost covered in silvery bloodstains glided by with a terrifying grin.

"Look, it's the Bloody Baron!" a Gryffindor girl pointed out in awe.

Percy waved a hand politely at the ghost hovering over their table. "Hello, Sir Nicholas. Have a nice summer?"

Sir Nicholas sighed deeply, floating down a bit lower. "Dismal. Once again, my request to join the headless hunt has been denied." He shook his head solemnly and began to turn away.

Ron blinked, his mouth half-open. "I know you! You're Nearly Headless Nick!"

Sir Nicholas sniffed, turning back with a air of offended dignity. "I prefer Sir Nicholas, if you don't mind."

Hermione, sitting across from them, immediately pushed her plate aside and chimed in, her voice full of intellectual curiosity. "'Nearly' headless? How can you be nearly headless?"

Harry rolled his eyes slightly at her bluntness, but Sir Nicholas merely narrowed his eyes. "Like this," he said dramatically.

Sir Nicholas grabbed his left ear and pulled his head sharply to the side. With a sickening *squelch*, his entire head swung right off his neck, hanging on by a microscopic thread of ghost skin and resting flat against his collarbone.

"Ahh!" Ron screamed, ducking away.

Hermione let out a soft moan of utter disgust, covering her face. Beside them, Ruhi—accustomed to divine, flawless cosmic beauty—was so completely thrown off by the sudden, bizarre sight that a massive, comical anime-style sweatdrop practically materialized on the side of her forehead as her eyes blinked in stunned disbelief. Harry, on the other hand, just simply stared in deadpan shock.

Satisfied with their reactions, Sir Nicholas snapped his head right back onto his shoulders with a pop. "Right then!" he hummed, gliding off toward the upper table.

---

A short while later, after the feast concluded, Percy stood up and began calling out to the new Gryffindor students.

"Gryffindors, follow me, please. Keep up. Thank you," Percy instructed, marching toward the exit of the Great Hall.

Nearby, a girl’s voice echoed, "Ravenclaws, follow me. This way."

Percy led the group through the massive stone corridors and into a grand, central tower. "This is the most direct path to the dormitories. Oh, and keep an eye on the staircases. They like to change."

The camera panned high up into the massive, echoing vertical chamber. A vast amount of grand stone staircases crisscrossed the open air, holding dozens of moving students. Right before their very eyes, a massive flight of stairs began to groan and swing slowly through the air, shifting its landing from one floor to another.

"Keep up, please, and follow me. Quickly now, come on. Come on," Percy urged, leading them onto the steps.

As they climbed, the framed oil paintings lining the walls began to shift, wave, and converse with one another.

"Seamus, that picture's moving!" Neville pointed out, mesmerized.

Ron nudged Harry, pointing at a portrait of a noblewoman who was smiling and waving at them. "Look at that one, Harry!"

Harry grinned mischief-filled eyes. "I think she fancies you, Ron."

A young Gryffindor girl gasped, pointing at a portrait of a handsome wizard in robes. "Oh, look! Look! Who's that girl?"

The man in the painting bowed gracefully to the passing students. "Welcome to Hogwarts."

"Who's that?" the girl giggled to her friend.

Ruhi walked alongside Harry, observing the enchanted oil paintings. "The magic in this castle is woven directly into its very stone," she remarked softly to Harry. "It breathes like a living entity."

Harry turned to her, smiling warmly. "It really does. It's unlike anything I've ever seen."

Finally, they reached the seventh floor, proceeding down a quiet corridor lined with torches. At the far end hung a massive, round oil painting of a plump woman wearing an elaborate pink silk dress.

"Password?" The Fat Lady asked cheerfully, fanning herself.

"Caput Draconis," Percy stated clearly.

The Fat Lady smiled warmly and nodded in confirmation. The entire heavy picture frame swung outward like a door, revealing a large circular opening in the stone wall leading directly into Gryffindor Tower.

"Follow me, everyone. Keep up. Quickly, come on," Percy called out, stepping through.

"Oh, wow," a girl breathed as they all stepped inside.

"Gather around here," Percy announced, gesturing to the cozy, rich room illuminated by a roaring fireplace, plush red armchairs, and tapestries. "Welcome to the Gryffindor Common Room. Boys' dormitory is upstairs and down to your left. Girls, the same on your right. You'll find that your belongings have already been brought up."

---

Later that night, long after the rest of the castle had fallen asleep, the tower was silent save for the crackle of dying embers.

In the boys' dormitory, Harry was sitting quietly on a wide stone window ledge in his blue striped pajamas. Outside, a gentle Scottish mist blanketed the moonlit grounds. Sitting right beside him on the ledge was his snowy owl, Hedwig.

Just a few feet away, standing near the archway of the adjoining landing, was Ruhi. She wore a simple, elegant golden-trimmed night robe, looking out the exact same massive stained-glass window. Perched safely on her forearm was Agni, her baby serpentine dragon. The wingless, long-bodied dragon glided softly through the air without wings, coiling around her wrist like a gleaming ruby bracelet before resting his head against her palm.

Ruhi stroked Agni's small scales gently, her golden-brown eyes fixed on the distant, dark forest. Her mind drifted to her swargwasi mother, Nageshwari. *Maa... I am finally here,* she thought softly, a faint ache in her chest. *I will find the truth.*

Harry looked over from Hedwig to Ruhi. Sensing her quiet solitude, he gently reached out and petted Hedwig's feathers, breaking the silence with a soft, comforting tone.

"Are you missing home, Ruhi?" Harry asked softly.

Ruhi turned her head, her dark hair falling gracefully over her shoulder. She offered him a gentle, genuine smile. "A part of me always remains in India, with my Gurumaa and the memory of my mother. But..." She looked out at the majestic, moonlit grounds of Hogwarts, feeling the strange, comforting pull of this place. "...I feel a sense of purpose here."

Harry nodded deeply, his green eyes reflecting the moonlight. "Me too. For the first time... I feel like I actually belong somewhere."

Both Ruhi and Harry looked out the window together, taking a deep, synchronized breath of the cool night air. A profound sense of peace washed over them both, knowing that despite the dark mysteries ahead, they would love it here at Hogwarts.

Ruhi turned back to look at Harry, and Harry turned to look at her at the exact same moment. Their eyes met, and a tender, glowing smile spread across both their faces—a soft, unspoken spark floating between their young souls, laying down the quiet, beautiful seeds of a deep affection that would grow for years to come.
