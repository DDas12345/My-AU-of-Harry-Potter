The dungeons were cold, dark, and damp, illuminated only by the faint green glow of bubbling liquid in brass cauldrons lined along stone tables. Glass jars containing preserved mystical ingredients—pickled herbs, dried eyes, and coiled serpent scales—stared down from the shadowy shelves lining the walls.

The Gryffindor and Slytherin first-years were whispering nervously among themselves, sitting in pairs near their assigned work stations. Ruhi sat alongside Hermione, her posture straight and composed, while Harry and Ron occupied the table right beside them.

Suddenly, the heavy wooden dungeon doors slammed open with a sharp, echoing *BANG!*

Professor Severus Snape swept into the room like a giant, dark bat, his black robes billowing dramatically behind him as he marched toward the front chalkboard. He spun around, his dark, piercing eyes sweeping across the trembling students with absolute, cold authority.

"There will be no foolish wand-waving or silly incantations in this class," Snape began, his voice barely above a whisper, yet carrying a terrifying intensity that instantly commanded complete silence. "As such, I don't expect many of you to appreciate the subtle science and exact art that is potion-making."

He took a slow step forward, his dark gaze flickering briefly toward the Slytherin table. "However, for those select few..." He paused, looking at Draco Malfoy, who offered a self-satisfied, smug smile. "...who possess the predisposition, I can teach you how to bewitch the mind and ensnare the senses. I can tell you how to bottle fame, brew glory, and even put a stopper..."

Draco raised his eyebrows in fascination, leaning in to listen.

"...in death," Snape finished smoothly.

As he spoke, Snape’s sharp eyes caught sight of Harry, who had his head lowered, feverishly scribbling down every single word of Snape's speech on a fresh piece of parchment so he wouldn't forget it. To Snape, however, it appeared as though the boy was daydreaming and ignoring his lecture.

Snape’s brow furrowed, a cold sneer forming on his lips. "Then again, maybe some of you have come to Hogwarts in possession of abilities so formidable that you feel confident enough to not pay attention."

Noticing the dark shift in the room, Hermione gently nudged Harry with her elbow. Harry snapped his head up, his green eyes widening as he realized Snape was staring directly down at him. Snape slowly glided across the stone floor, stopping right in front of Harry and Ron's table.

"Mr. Potter," Snape drawled softly, his tone dripping with sarcasm. "Our new... celebrity. Tell me, what would I get if I added powdered root of asphodel to an infusion of wormwood?"

Hermione’s hand shot straight up into the air, her fingers trembling with eager knowledge. Harry looked blankly at Snape, his mind racing. He gave a small, helpless shrug.

"You don't know?" Snape questioned softly, a mock trace of disappointment in his voice. "Well, let's try again. Where, Mr. Potter, would you look if I asked you to find me a bezoar?"

Hermione’s hand stretched even higher toward the ceiling, nearly lifting her out of her wooden seat.

Harry swallowed hard. "I don't know, sir."

"And what is the difference between monkshood and wolfsbane?" Snape pressed, stepping even closer.

"I don't know, sir," Harry repeated quietly.

Snape folded his arms inside his heavy black sleeves, looking down his long nose at Harry. "Pity. Clearly, fame isn't everything."

Across the aisle, Draco Malfoy let out a quiet, mocking snicker, a triumphant smirk plastering his pale face.

Ruhi’s golden-brown eyes snapped to Draco. A flash of fiery, royal indignation burned within her. She loathed bullies who derived joy from watching others be humiliated, and she was certainly not going to sit silently while Harry was unfairly targeted.

Snape leaned down slightly, his voice cold. "Is it, Mr. Potter?"

Before Harry could swallow his pride and accept the humiliation, Ruhi leaned over to Harry and whispered urgently, "Harry, why didn't you defend yourself?"

Without waiting for his answer, Ruhi turned her head straight toward the feared Potions Master. Her voice rang out clear, smooth, and resonant, shattering the heavy silence of the dungeon.

"Professor Snape, Harry was not ignoring your lecture," Ruhi stated firmly, her posture regal and completely devoid of fear. "He was diligently writing down every word of your speech on his parchment so he could study it."

A collective gasp echoed across the room. No first-year had ever dared to talk back to Severus Snape!

Hermione’s face turned white with sheer panic. She reached over and tugged frantically on Ruhi’s sleeve, whispering in an anxious rush, "Ruhi, don't intervene! You'll get us into trouble!"

Ruhi didn't flinch. She gently patted Hermione’s hand while keeping her steady gaze locked onto Snape. "Professor Snape has misunderstood Harry, Hermione. I am simply clarifying the truth."

Snape slowly turned his body toward Ruhi. His dark eyes flickered with a sudden, deep spark. He knew her true identity as the Ichhadhaari Naagin heiress of Nageshwari, and seeing her bold, fearless defense of the boy left him secretly impressed by her unyielding spirit. A faint, uncharacteristic smirk touched the corner of Snape's lips.

"Is that so, Miss Ruhi?" Snape drawled, stepping toward her table. "If you are so eager to speak for Mr. Potter, let us see if your mind matches your confidence. Tell me, what is the primary properties of the Draught of Living Death, and which rare ingredient stabilizes its brew?"

Beside her, Hermione’s hand exploded into the air once again, stretching so high her shoulder nearly popped out of its socket, desperate to answer correctly.

Ruhi knew in her heart that she had not yet studied this complex European potion in depth. The exact ingredient escaped her, but as a future queen trained in royal diplomacy and spiritual mastery, she knew that true authority lay in how one carried their voice.

Ruhi took a calm, deep breath. She sat up straight, lifted her chin, and looked directly into Snape's eyes. In a voice dripping with absolute, unwavering confidence and regal authority—making her words sound completely factual—she answered:

"The Draught of Living Death brings a person into a sleep so profound it mirrors the stillness of the grave, Professor. And its volatile nature is stabilized through the essence of the crushed Sopophorous bean, combined with the pure harmony of the brewer's intent."

The moment the words left her mouth, Hermione stretched her hand even higher—practically standing on her tiptoes—and blurted out, "Professor! The Sopophorous bean juice is correct, but it must be cut, not crushed, to release its essence!"

Hermione was eager to correct Ruhi’s minor technical error, but Snape didn't even look at Hermione. His dark eyes remained fixed on Ruhi. He recognized that while her exact technique was slightly off, her innate understanding of potion philosophy and her supreme, unshakable confidence were the marks of a true leader.

A thin, satisfied smirk crossed Snape’s face.

"An unconventional answer, Miss Ruhi," Snape announced, his voice echoing clearly through the dungeon. "Yet delivered with the absolute composure of a true master. Five points... to Gryffindor. For demonstrating remarkable confidence and standing for your peer."

The entire room sat in stunned silence. Gryffindors gaining points in Snape's very first class was virtually unheard of!

Draco’s smirk vanished instantly, replaced by a look of utter disbelief. Harry turned to Ruhi, his eyes shining with immense gratitude and admiration, a warm flush rising to his cheeks. Ruhi offered Harry a soft, triumphant smile, her golden-brown eyes sparkling under the dim dungeon candlelight.
