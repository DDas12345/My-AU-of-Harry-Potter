The rhythmic, hypnotic *clack-clack, clack-clack* of the Hogwarts Express echoed through the train compartment as the scarlet locomotive left the bustling city of London far behind. Outside the window, the scenery shifted into a beautiful, rolling expanse of green British countryside, dotted with grazing sheep and distant, misty hills.

Inside the cozy compartment, Harry and Ruhi sat opposite each other. Ruhi had placed Agni’s cage securely on the seat beside her, the silk cloth draped carefully over it so the little dragon could sleep away the chilly drafts. She sat with her spine perfectly straight, observing the unfamiliar landscape with sharp, calculating eyes. Harry was leaning back against the cushioned seat, staring out the window with an expression of pure, unadulterated content. For the first time in his life, he was away from the Dursleys, heading toward a place where he might finally belong.

Suddenly, the heavy glass door of their compartment slid open with a soft rattle.

The gangly, red-haired boy they had met at the barrier, Ron Weasley, stood in the doorway. He looked a bit flustered, clutching a tattered old jacket to his chest, his face slightly flushed from walking up and down the long train corridor.

"Excuse me, do you mind?" Ron asked, pointing a bit nervously into the compartment. "Everywhere else is full."

Ruhi looked up, her expression instantly softening into a polite, welcoming smile. "Sure," she said smoothly.

At the exact same fraction of a second, Harry turned his head and chimed in, "Not at all."

The two sentences spliced together perfectly. Ruhi and Harry blinked, looked at each other, and instantly burst into a quiet, synchronized giggle. It was a bizarre, amusing moment of shared intuition, as if they had unconsciously completed each other’s thoughts. Ron blinked at them, a bit confused by the sudden laughter, but he smiled nonetheless and slid into the compartment, taking the empty seat right across from Harry.

"I'm Ron, by the way," the red-haired boy introduced himself, stowing his jacket away. "Ron Weasley."

Ruhi inclined her head with the natural, regal grace of a future queen, though she kept her tone warm and approachable. "It is a pleasure to meet you properly, Ron. I am Ruhi, and I have come all the way from India to attend Hogwarts."

Ron’s eyes widened slightly in fascination. "Wow, India? That’s brilliant! I didn't know Hogwarts took students from that far away."

Harry then turned to Ron, offering a friendly grin. "And I'm Harry. Harry Potter."

The casual introduction hit Ron like a physical shock wave. His jaw dropped completely agape, his eyes widening to the size of Galleons. He stared at Harry as if a ghost had just materialized on the seat.

"So—so it's true!" Ron stammered, his voice cracking slightly with excitement. "I mean, do you really have the... the...?"

Harry tilted his head, a familiar, slight awkwardness creeping back into his posture. "The what?"

Ron leaned forward, dropping his voice to a theatrical, reverent whisper. "The *scar*?"

"Oh," Harry muttered, a small, self-conscious smile playing on his lips. He reached up with his fingers and gently lifted the thick bangs of his messy black hair, pulling them away from his forehead.

There, etched sharply into his skin, was the distinct, jagged shape of a lightning bolt.

Ron gasped, staring at it in absolute rapture. "Wicked!"

While Ron was staring in awe, Ruhi was looking at the lightning bolt with a deep, silent intensity. Her sharp, ancient Naagin instincts immediately sensed a strange, residual energy hum within that scar. It wasn't the pure, divine energy she was used to in the Shiva Mandir; it felt dark, fragmented, and heavy. Yet, she didn't understand the boy's sheer fanaticism over a physical wound.

Ruhi tilted her head, her brow furrowing slightly in genuine confusion. "Forgive my ignorance, Ron," she spoke up, drawing both boys' attention. "But what is the specialty of this scar? Why does it cause such a reaction?"

Ron froze, his head snapping toward Ruhi. He looked utterly, profoundly shocked, as if she had just asked him why the sun shines or why water is wet. "You... you don't know?" he gasped, his voice squeaking. "Ruhi, you don't know who he is? He’s Harry Potter! He’s famous in the entire wizarding world!"

Ruhi calmly shook her head. "In India, our histories and our legends are quite different. I am afraid I am unfamiliar with British wizarding history."

Ron immediately leaned in, eager to share the legendary tale. "Blimey! Well, Harry is the Only One Who Lived! Ten years ago, there was this really bad, dark wizard... You-Know-Who. He was terrifying, Ruhi. He killed everyone who stood against him, powerful witches and wizards, everyone! But then, he tried to kill Harry when Harry was just a baby. And You-Know-Who's curse rebounded! It destroyed his powers, made him disappear, and all Harry got was that lightning scar. Harry stopped the darkest wizard of all time!"

Ruhi sat perfectly still, but beneath her calm exterior, her heart began to hammer violently against her ribs. *A dark, powerful wizard who killed ruthlessly ten years ago.* The timeline matched perfectly with the murder of her mother, Nageshwari.

She masked her sudden surge of emotion flawlessly, keeping her expression neutral, though her golden-brown eyes darkened with an intense, quiet focus. She looked at Harry, a newfound respect and a deep, tragic understanding dawning within her. He, too, had been orphaned by the exact same monstrous darkness that she was hunting. They were bound by the same shadowy enemy.

Before she could delve deeper into her thoughts, a loud, cheerful rattling sound echoed from the corridor. The heavy compartment door slid open once more, revealing a kindly, dimpled woman pushing a massive, tiered trolley stacked to the brim with colorful, magical confections. There were stacks of Cauldron Cakes, boxes of Bertie Bott’s Every Flavour Beans, Pumpkin Pasties, and chocolate frogs that seemed to twitch inside their packaging.

"Anything off the trolley, dears?" the woman asked warmly, offering them a sweet smile.

Ron slumped back into his seat, his face falling slightly. He reached into his pocket and pulled out a plastic-wrapped package containing a couple of dry, slightly squished, pale-looking sandwiches. He held them up with a forced, brave smile, though his stomach gave a treacherous rumble. "No, thanks. I'm all set," he said, smacking his lips unconvincingly.

Harry immediately felt a pang of sympathy for Ron. Eager to share his newfound wealth, Harry reached deep into his pockets and pulled out a large, heavy handful of shining golden Galleons, silver Sickles, and bronze Knuts that he had retrieved from Gringotts.

Beside him, Ruhi realized she also needed to pay if she wanted to experience these western sweets. She reached into a small, beautifully embroidered silk pouch tied to her waist. When she withdrew her hand, the compartment was instantly illuminated by a brilliant, dazzling sparkle.

Resting in Ruhi's delicate palm were a cluster of flawless, uncut mini diamonds, shimmering freshwater pearls, and a couple of rare, radiant gemstones that glowed with a faint, inner light.

Ron looked from Harry’s pile of gold coins to Ruhi’s glittering palm, and his eyes nearly popped right out of his skull. His mouth fell open so wide a Chocolate Frog could have jumped right in. He had never seen that much raw, priceless treasure in his entire life.

Little did Harry and Ron know, Ruhi's treasures had been taken directly from the ancient, boundless vaults of Naaglok. Her beloved Gurumaa, Uttara Maa, knew that the human and wizarding worlds required physical currency to trade. Thus, she had gifted Ruhi a small pouch of royal gems—a minuscule fraction of the Naaglok treasure, yet valuable enough that even the smallest diamond in her hand could literally buy the entire Hogwarts Express, if not the whole wizarding village! Uttara Maa had smiled when giving it to her, knowing full well that her disciplined, righteous future Naagrani would never abuse such wealth.

Harry looked at Ruhi's diamonds in awe, then looked up at the trolley woman, a massive, excited grin spreading across his face. Ruhi looked at the array of colorful sweets, an amused spark in her eyes.

In perfect, flawless unison, both Harry and Ruhi looked at the woman and declared:

"We'll take the lot!"

The exact same words, spoken at the exact same pitch, left their mouths simultaneously.

The trolley woman chuckled warmly, while Harry and Ruhi snapped their heads to look at one another. A beat of silence passed before they both leaned back and began to giggle uncontrollably once again. This time, the laughter was louder, warmer, and completely shattered any remaining awkwardness in the air.

Ron could only stare at his two extraordinary new compartment-mates, his squished sandwiches completely forgotten, realizing that this journey to Hogwarts was going to be far more legendary than he had ever dreamed.
