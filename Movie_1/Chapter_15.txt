The four Gryffindors were making their way up a massive flight of stone steps in the Grand Staircase tower. The high, vertical chamber echoed with the distant hum of moving portraits and shifting stone. As they climbed, a heavy stone railing pulled back into the wall with a hollow *click*. Hermione glanced at it briefly, completely unfazed, and continued walking.

Ron shook his head, turning to Harry. "I'm telling you, it's spooky. She knows more about you than you do."

Harry let out a dry, quiet sigh. "Who doesn't?"

Without warning, the entire staircase beneath their feet gave a violent shudder. A low, grinding groan of ancient mechanisms echoed through the shaft as the heavy stone structure began to swing slowly through the open air.

"Ahh!" Ron yelped, instantly grabbing the cold stone railing.

Hermione gasped, her hand flying to her chest.

Harry gripped the balustrade, looking around in alarm. "What's happening?"

"The staircases change, remember?" Hermione reminded them, straining to keep her balance.

Ruhi stood perfectly upright, her posture fluid and centered despite the moving floor. Her golden-brown eyes scanned the shifting archways above and below them. "This staircase is a living puzzle," Ruhi noted calmly, her voice carrying a sharp, tactical focus. "If one is not careful, it can become the biggest obstacle in reaching safety."

With a final heavy thud, the staircase locked into place against a shadowed landing, opening a path to a quiet, unfamiliar hallway.

Harry looked down the passage and turned to Ron. "Let's go this way."

"Before the staircase moves again," Ron agreed hastily.

They pushed open a heavy oak door and stepped out onto the landing. The corridor ahead was eerie, pitch-black, and thoroughly deserted. Cobwebs draped across the vaulted ceiling, and a cold draft whistled through the stone pillars.

Ron slowed his pace, looking around nervously. "Does anyone feel like we shouldn't be here?"

Hermione stopped dead in her tracks, her eyes widening as she recognized the layout. "We're *not* supposed to be here. This is the third floor. It's forbidden."

Before anyone could turn back, a torch mounted on a tall stone support abruptly flared to life with a loud *WHOOSH*.

From the shadows near the base of the pillar, a scrawny, dust-colored cat with glowing, lamp-like red eyes stepped into the light. It was Mrs. Norris. The cat let out a sharp, piercing *MEOW*, staring directly at the four intruders.

The group jumped back in collective surprise.

Harry’s eyes darted down the dark hallway. "Let's go!"

Mrs. Norris let out another harsh meow, her tail twitching in alarm.

"It's Filch's cat!" Hermione hissed.

"Run!" Harry shouted.

In that fraction of a second, Ruhi’s sharp instincts took over. Her golden-brown eyes flared, glowing with a brilliant, supernatural gold that illuminated the dim corridor.

The four Gryffindors sprinted down the long hallway. As their boots slapped against the stone, iron braziers mounted along the walls ignited one by one in rapid succession, casting long, frantic shadows behind them. They reached the dead end of the corridor, coming face-to-face with a thick, iron-reinforced wooden door.

Harry grabbed the heavy brass handle and tugged with all his might, but the mechanism refused to budge. "Quick! Let's hide through that door!" He yanked it again in sheer desperation. "It's locked!"

Ron threw his hands up in panic. "That's it! We're done for!"

"Oh, move over!" Hermione snapped, pushing her way to the front. She whipped out her vine-wood wand, pointing it precisely at the keyhole. "*Alohomora.*"

A sharp, metallic click echoed from inside the lock. The heavy latch lifted automatically, and the door creaked open.

"Get in!" Hermione ordered, shoving them through the gap before slipping in last and pulling the door shut behind them.

Inside the dark room, Ron blinked, staring at her in disbelief. "*Alohomora*?"

Hermione smoothed down her robes, her tone crisp and matter-of-fact. "*Standard Book of Spells*, Chapter 7."

On the other side of the heavy door, soft, dragging footsteps echoed down the stone corridor. Argus Filch stopped right outside, holding up his flickering lantern. Mrs. Norris rubbed against his ankles, letting out a low meow.

Filch leaned down, his raspy voice filtering through the wood. "Anyone here, my sweet?"

Mrs. Norris meowed once more, sniffing the air.

Filch grunted, turning on his heel. "Come on." Their footsteps slowly faded away down the hall.

Inside the dark chamber, Hermione pressed her ear against the wood, listening intently. "Filch is gone."

Ron let out a massive breath of relief, leaning back against the wall. "Probably thinks this door's locked."

"It *was* locked," Hermione pointed out coldly.

Harry’s green eyes were fixed on the shadowy darkness ahead of them, his voice dropping into a tense whisper. "And for good reason."

Ron and Hermione turned around to stand beside Harry and Ruhi.

Resting on a raised wooden platform in the center of the chamber was a creature of absolute nightmare. It was a monstrous, colossal three-headed dog, its massive paws stretching across the entire floorboards. The beast’s ears twitched as it began to stir from its slumber. It let out a deep, chest-rattling growl, yawned to reveal rows of razor-sharp teeth dripping with thick saliva, and locked six glowing eyes directly onto the four first-years.

"AHHHHHHH!" all four screamed in sheer terror.

The monstrous dog reared back, all three heads opening their jaws wide to let out a deafening, bone-shattering roar!

Before the beast could strike, Ruhi stepped directly between the monster and her friends. Her ancient warrior instincts completely overrode her caution. Opening her mouth, a thin, split serpent tongue flickered out with lightning speed. She executed a swift, fluid strike toward the air, projecting a concentrated burst of her Ichhadhaari Naagin *Nidra Vish*—her sacred, sleep-inducing mystical venom—directly into the monster's nostrils!

An invisible, shimmering golden mist hit the three-headed dog instantly.

The colossal beast froze mid-roar. Its six eyes went heavy, its massive jaws let out a slow, massive yawn, and its legs buckled beneath its immense weight. With a heavy *THUD* that shook the stone floor, all three heads collapsed onto the wooden planks, fast asleep and snoring softly.

Harry, Hermione, and Ron stood rooted to the spot, their jaws dropped in absolute, speechless shock.

Ron stared at Ruhi as if she had just transformed into a dragon. "What... what in the name of Merlin was *that*?!"

Ruhi snapped her head around, her golden-brown eyes returning to normal as her face set into a firm, urgent expression. "This is not the time, Ron! Out! Now!"

Without waiting another second, Harry grabbed the door handle, threw it open, and all four scrambled out into the corridor. Hermione slammed the heavy door shut, grabbed her wand, and cast a locking charm before they sprinted at full speed down the third-floor hallway, not daring to look back until they reached the safety of the Gryffindor Tower.

---

A few minutes later, the portrait of the Fat Lady swung shut behind them as the four tumbled into the warm, quiet Gryffindor Common Room.

The roaring fireplace cast long, flickering orange shadows across the plush armchairs. The room was completely empty, the rest of the house having long since gone to bed.

Ron collapsed onto a soft velvet armchair, clutching his chest and panting heavily, his face completely drained of color. Harry leaned against the stone mantelpiece, catching his breath as his heart hammered against his ribs. Hermione sank onto the edge of a sofa, wringing her hands nervously while her mind raced to rationalize what had just transpired.

Ruhi stood quietly near the center of the room. Though her breath was calm and her posture regal, her mind was calculating every detail. She had used her *Nidra Vish* to protect her friends, but she knew she had raised massive questions.

Ron wiped his sweaty forehead, looking up at Ruhi with wide, bewildered eyes. "Bloody hell... Ruhi! You—you just put a giant, three-headed monster to sleep with... with some kind of serpent tongue! I saw it! What was that magic?!"

Hermione snapped her head up, her analytical mind piecing together the visual clues. "I've read about ancient Eastern mystical techniques, but I've never seen anything like that! It wasn't a wand spell! There was no incantation! Ruhi, how did you do that without a wand? And why did your eyes glow gold back in the hallway?"

Harry stepped forward, his green eyes filled not with fear, but with intense curiosity and deep concern for his friend. "Ruhi... what kind of power do you have? You saved our lives back there, but... how?"

Ruhi took a slow, deep breath. She looked at the three eager, anxious faces staring back at her. She knew she could not reveal her true identity as the royal Ichhadhaari Naagin heiress of Naaglok, nor could she expose her mother Nageshwari’s tragic past or her secret revenge mission to uncover Voldemort’s dark history at Hogwarts. Keeping her identity concealed was vital for her safety and the success of her quest.

Ruhi held out her hands gracefully, offering them a calm, reassuring smile that radiated warm, ancient wisdom.

"In my homeland of India, magic is not confined merely to wooden wands and spoken Latin incantations," Ruhi explained smoothly, her voice rich with unwavering confidence. "From a very young age, under the guidance of my Gurumaa, I was trained in the ancient, sacred arts of elemental energy and spiritual defense. The serpent is a sacred, revered symbol of cosmic power and protection in our culture. What you witnessed was a specialized, non-verbal martial art—a spiritual breath technique passed down in my family to neutralize aggression and grant peaceful sleep to dangerous beasts."

Ron blinked, his mouth slightly open. "A... a spiritual breath technique? You can just knock out a giant monster by breathing at it?!"

"It requires absolute focus and years of rigorous discipline," Ruhi added with a graceful nod, keeping her cover story flawless. "I apologize if it startled you, but my priority was to ensure all three of you remained unharmed."

Harry’s expression softened completely. A wave of immense gratitude and admiration washed over him. He stepped closer to Ruhi, looking straight into her eyes. "You don't need to apologize, Ruhi. You saved us. If you hadn't done that, that beast would have ripped us to shreds."

Hermione let out a long sigh, running a hand through her bushy hair as her mind accepted the logical cultural explanation. "It's... it's extraordinary. Eastern non-wand discipline is mentioned in advanced theory, but seeing it in action is astounding."

She then shook her head, her tone shifting into pure outrage as she remembered the chamber itself. "But what on earth is the school doing keeping a thing like that locked up in a castle full of students?!"

"Didn't you see what it was standing on?" Hermione added, standing up.

Ron frowned, still recovering. "I wasn't looking at its feet! I was a bit preoccupied with its heads!"

"It was standing on a trapdoor," Ruhi stated clearly, her sharp eyes having analyzed the entire room in those brief seconds. "Which means it is not being kept there as a pet. It is guarding something."

"Guarding something?" Harry repeated, his mind racing.

"That's right," Hermione agreed, turning toward the stairs leading to the girls' dormitory. "Now, if you two don't mind, I'm going to bed before either of you come up with another clever idea to get us killed. Or worse, expelled!"

She turned on her heel and marched up the staircase.

Ron stared after her in utter disbelief. "She needs to sort out her priorities!"

Harry let out a quiet laugh, turning to look at Ruhi once more. The room fell quiet, save for the crackle of the fire.

"Thank you, Ruhi," Harry whispered softly, his green eyes glowing with genuine warmth. "For protecting us."

Ruhi met his gaze, her golden-brown eyes shining with a soft, affectionate light. "Always, Harry. We are a team."

Ron gave a weary yawn, waving a hand as he trudged toward the boys' dormitory. "I'm going to bed before another monster shows up. Night, Ruhi. Night, Harry."

As Harry and Ruhi shared one final, sweet, lingering smile in the warm firelight, the mystery of the third-floor trapdoor burned bright in their minds, setting the stage for the dark adventure ahead.
