The bustling crowd at London’s King’s Cross Station was a chaotic sea of rushing commuters, echoing train whistles, and the heavy clatter of luggage trolleys. Standing amidst the noise was eleven-year-old Ruhi. She looked remarkably poised for her age, her dark hair neatly tied back, and her sharp, expressive eyes scanning the brick pillars between platforms nine and ten.

Pushed against her trolley was a sleek, dark travel cage covered partially by a silk cloth. Inside it, Agni the mini-dragon sat as quietly as he could, his tiny golden-red tail twitching with anxiety. Ruhi had used a powerful cloaking illusion from Naaglok to ensure that to any passing non-magical person, Agni looked like nothing more than a peculiar, exotic lizard. Still, the little dragon hissed softly under his breath, utterly overwhelmed by the cold British dampness and the loud, echoing noises of the train station.

Ruhi glanced down at her heavy parchment ticket. *Platform 9¾.* It made absolute mathematical nonsense in the human world, but she knew that European wizards loved hiding their secrets behind layers of illusions.

As she stood there calculating her next move, she noticed a boy a few feet away. He looked just as lost as she was. He was a skinny boy with messy black hair, wearing taped, oversized round spectacles, and pushing a trolley stacked high with trunks. In a cage atop his luggage sat a beautiful, snowy white owl that hooted softly. The boy was looking around hopelessly, his green eyes darting between the signs for platforms nine and ten.

Sensing a kindred spirit in confusion, Ruhi decided to approach him. Her footsteps were light, entirely silent—a natural habit of an Ichchhaari Naagin.

"Excuse me," Ruhi spoke up, her English smooth, flawless, and perfectly articulated. "Are you perhaps looking for something that shouldn't logically exist here either?"

The boy startled slightly, turning around to look at her. A look of immense relief washed over his face when he realized he wasn't the only one struggling. "Oh! Um, yeah, actually. I'm looking for Platform 9¾. Have you ever heard of it?"

"I am looking for the exact same thing," Ruhi replied with a polite, calm smile. She extended her hand gracefully. "I am Ruhi."

The boy quickly wiped his hand on his trousers and shook hers. "I'm Harry. Harry Potter."

He braced himself slightly, his shoulders tensing up automatically. Over the last few weeks, every single wizard or witch he had met in Diagon Alley had gasped, stared at his forehead, or treated him like some sort of mythical creature just because of his name. He expected this girl to do the same.

Instead, Ruhi merely nodded, her expression remaining completely serene and respectful. "It is a pleasure to meet you, Harry. Have you any idea how we are supposed to find a fraction of a platform?"

Harry blinked, momentarily stunned. He stared at her, waiting for the recognition, but there was none. She was looking at him just like a normal person—not a savior, not a legend, just a boy named Harry. A wave of profound relief washed over him, and his shoulders relaxed completely. It was incredibly refreshing to be treated normally for once.

"I have no idea," Harry admitted, offering a genuine smile. "I've been walking up and down, but the numbers just jump from nine straight to ten. By the way, what kind of bird is that in your cage? It looks... really unique."

Ruhi glanced at her cage, where Agni was currently glaring through the bars at Harry’s snowy owl. "Ah, this is Agni. He is a very rare breed from back home in India. He doesn't quite like the British weather."

Before Harry could ask anything more, the sound of a hurried, bustling group caught their attention. A plump, kind-faced woman with vibrant red hair was leading a small procession of red-headed children, all of them pushing heavy wooden trunks on trolleys just like theirs.

The woman was talking loudly as she maneuvered through the crowd. "It's the same year after year. Always packed with Muggles, of course..."

Harry and Ruhi looked at each other, their eyebrows raising simultaneously.

"Muggles?" Harry muttered, utterly confused.

"Muggles..." Ruhi repeated the word under her breath, analyzing it. Hagrid had explained the term to Harry, and Uttara Maa had warned Ruhi about the British term for non-magical people, but hearing it used so casually in a crowded station caught them both off guard.

"Come on," the red-haired woman called out to her children, completely unaware of the two eleven-year-olds watching them intently. "Platform 9¾ this way! All right, Percy, you first."

Harry and Ruhi stepped closer, watching with bated breath.

A tall, structured boy with red hair and a shiny badge on his chest stepped forward. He took a deep breath, gripped his trolley firmly, and began to run directly toward the solid brick wall separating platforms nine and ten. Harry held his breath, expecting a horrific crash.

But there was no crash. The boy ran straight into the solid brick, and amazingly, he disappeared right through it as if the wall were made of water.

Harry’s jaw dropped in absolute amazement. Ruhi’s eyes widened slightly, a flicker of genuine surprise crossing her features. She had seen advanced illusionary portals in Naaglok, but seeing western wizards use a physical brick wall as a gateway was certainly an interesting method.

The mother turned to the rest of her children. "Fred, you next."

A boy who looked identical to another boy next to him stepped forward, his face twisting into a mischievous grin. "He's not Fred, I am!"

The other twin chimed in seamlessly, "Honestly, woman, you call yourself our mother."

Mrs. Weasley looked momentarily flustered, waving her hand apologetically. "Oh, I'm sorry, George."

The boy stepped up to his trolley, aligning himself with the brick wall. Just before he ran, he looked back at his mother with a triumphant smirk. "I'm only joking. I am Fred."

Without waiting for her reaction, he sprinted forward and dashed right through the solid wall, followed immediately by his twin brother, George, who vanished into the brick just as quickly.

Harry shook his head in absolute disbelief, pinching his own arm slightly to make sure he wasn't dreaming. Beside him, Ruhi shook her head too, a subtle, amused smile playing on her lips.

"Well," Ruhi whispered, her voice laced with quiet resolve as she looked at the barrier. "It seems magic in this country requires a great deal of faith in solid masonry."

Harry let out a nervous laugh. "Yeah. It looks like we just have to run straight at a brick wall."
