The compartment was now an absolute wonderland of crumpled brightly colored wrappers, empty cardboard boxes, and half-eaten magical treats. Ruhi, Harry, and Ron were sitting closely together, surrounded by their massive haul. On Ron’s knee, his chubby gray rat, Scabbers, was precariously perched with an empty Bertie Bott’s box wedged awkwardly over his head, sniffing around for any leftover crumbs.

Harry picked up a colorful, striped little box, turning it over curiously in his hands. "Bertie Bott's Every Flavour Beans?" he read aloud.

"They mean every flavor!" Ron warned around a mouthful of Pumpkin Pasty. He leaned forward conspiratorially. "There's chocolate and peppermint, and there's also spinach, liver, and tripe. George swore he got a bogey-flavoured one once!"

Ruhi, who had just elegantly popped a shimmering, pearlescent purple bean into her mouth, froze. Harry, who was currently chewing a bright green one, stopped dead in his tracks. They exchanged a look of sheer, horrified realization. Simultaneously, Harry spat his bean out into his hand, and Ruhi, discarding her usual royal composure for a brief second, quickly took the bean out of her mouth with a delicate grimace. Coming from the pristine, pure environment of the Shiva Mandir, she had never in her wildest dreams expected that a sweet could deliberately taste like something foul. Harry gave her a sympathetic, slightly green look, and they both let out a small, breathless chuckle at their shared misfortune.

Trying to wash out the taste, Harry picked up a beautifully embossed blue and gold pentagonal package. "These aren't real frogs, are they?"

"It's just a spell," Ron explained casually, waving his hand. "Besides, it's the cards you want. Each pack's got a famous witch or wizard. I got about 500 myself."

Harry broke the golden seal of his package. The moment the lid popped open, a large, glistening dark-brown chocolate frog gave a powerful leap right out of the box. It landed squarely on Harry’s forearm, scrambled up his shirt, and leaped onto the glass windowpane, using its sticky webbed feet to climb upward.

"Watch it!" Ron shouted, pointing frantically.

The frog reached the small open gap at the top of the train window. With one final, energetic contraction of its legs, it launched itself out into the rushing, windy countryside.

"Oh, that's rotten luck," Ron sighed, shaking his head in disappointment. "They've only got one good jump in them to begin with."

Harry looked down at the stiff piece of cardboard remaining in his box. The portrait showed a wise old man with half-moon spectacles, a long silver beard, and a magnificent sweeping robe. "I've got Dumbledore!" Harry said, his face lighting up.

"I got about six of him," Ron muttered, shuffling through some of his own cards.

Meanwhile, Ruhi gracefully picked up her own unopened Chocolate Frog box. She peeled back the golden foil, paid no mind to her own chocolate frog—which immediately leapt onto the ceiling and escaped out the door—and pulled out the moving holographic card.

The moment her eyes fell upon the portrait, the breath completely left her lungs. Her fingers trembled against the gold-embossed border.

Glancing back at her from the magical photograph was a stunningly beautiful woman with long, cascading dark hair, piercing, regal eyes, and a profound, divine grace that Ruhi knew better than her own reflection. The caption at the bottom read in elegant gold script: **NAGESHWARI**.

It was her mother. Her late, beloved *maa*.

Ruhi’s heart hammered against her ribs like a trapped bird. A wave of profound grief and sudden, scorching fury threatened to break through her facade, her inner Naagin scales rippling violently beneath her golden lehenga jacket. But her years of rigorous mental training under Uttara Maa instantly kicked in. She locked her emotions deep down into a vault within her soul. She could not break. She could not reveal her true identity or her ultimate motive to anyone yet.

Ron glanced over at Ruhi's card and his eyes went wide, his mouth dropping open. "Blimey, Ruhi! You got Nageshwari! That’s an incredibly rare find, that is!"

Ruhi forced her voice to remain perfectly smooth, though her eyes remained locked on the image of her mother waving gently from the card. "Is she well-known in your world, Ron?"

"Oh, absolutely!" Ron said, leaning in, his face full of genuine awe. "She was a legendary shape-shifting serpent from India—an Ichchhaari Naagin, they called her. But she mysteriously died a few years back. No one really knows what happened; some say she was murdered by dark forces, others say she just vanished into thin air. But she was brilliant! She did a massive amount of work for Hogwarts, including huge charitable work, donating ancient magical artifacts to the school, and helping protect the castle's older wards. My dad says she was one of the most powerful and kindest magical beings to ever visit Britain."

Hearing her mother praised by a complete stranger in a foreign land brought a bittersweet ache to Ruhi's chest. Her mother had given so much to this world, only to be brutally violated and slaughtered by its darkest monster.

Harry, sensing a sudden shift in the air, looked from his card to hers. But as he looked down at his own card, he gasped. The space where Albus Dumbledore had been smiling was now completely blank. Ruhi looked down at her card too—Nageshwari had disappeared from the frame, leaving only an empty backdrop of an Indian palace.

"Hey, he's gone!" Harry exclaimed.

"She's gone too," Ruhi echoed, her voice quiet but sharp.

Ron looked at them both like they were a bit mad. "Well, you can't expect them to hang around all day, can you? They've got things to do!"

Just then, Scabbers gave a loud, pathetic squeak on Ron's knee, kicking his hind legs. Ron looked down at him, his expression turning a bit sour. "This is Scabbers, by the way. Pathetic, isn't he?"

"Just a little bit," Harry agreed honestly, looking at the ragged, sleeping rodent.

Ruhi, however, frowned softly. As the future Sheshnaagrani, she held a deep reverence for all living creatures, no matter how small or seemingly insignificant. Her sharp eyes scanned the rat. Strangely, her primordial serpent instincts felt a bizarre, unsettling ripple from the rodent—an unnatural aura that felt far too heavy for a simple pet.

Hiding her suspicion, Ruhi looked at Ron and said softly, "You shouldn't say this, Ron. He is your companion, and every creature deserves respect and care."

Ron’s face flushed a deep pink, matching his hair. He rubbed the back of his neck and smiled sheepishly, feeling a bit reprimanded by Ruhi's mature, dignified demeanor. Eager to change the subject and impress his new friends, he snatched the box off Scabbers' head.

"Right, um... well, Fred gave me a spell to turn him yellow. Want to see?" Ron asked eagerly.

Harry’s eyes brightened. "Yeah!"

Ruhi inclined her head, her curiosity piqued despite her underlying suspicions. "I would also like to see this."

Ron cleared his throat loudly, puffing out his chest. He fished a chipped, battered old wand out of his trunk, a bit of white unicorn hair sticking precariously out of its tip. He raised the wand, pointing it directly at the plump gray rat. "Ahem. Sun—"

Before he could utter another syllable, the heavy compartment door slid open with a sharp, decisive *snap*.

Standing in the doorway was a young girl with an abundance of bushy brown hair, exceptionally large front teeth, and an incredibly bossy, self-assured voice. She was already wearing her pristine black Hogwarts school robes.

"Has anyone seen a toad? A boy named Neville has lost one," she announced rapidly, her tone demanding answers.

"No," Ron replied bluntly, blinking at the sudden intrusion.

The girl’s eyes immediately fell upon the raised wand in Ron's hand. Her eyebrows shot up. "Oh, are you doing magic? Let's see then."

Ron looked a bit taken aback, glancing at Harry and Ruhi before clearing his throat once more, trying to regain his confidence. He took a dramatic stance and swung his wand downward.

"Sunshine, daisies, butter mellow, turn this stupid fat rat yellow!"

A small orange spark fizzled weakly from the tip of his cracked wand. A dull *pop* echoed through the compartment, but absolutely nothing happened. Scabbers merely squeaked, turned around, and went right back to sleep. Ron shrugged, looking thoroughly embarrassed.

The bushy-haired girl sniffed disdainfully, crossing her arms. "Are you sure that's a real spell? Well, it's not very good, is it? Of course, I've only tried a few simple ones myself, but they've all worked for me."

Without asking for permission, she marched right into the compartment, stepping past Ron, and sat down directly across from Harry. She pulled out a sleek, elegant vine-wood wand from her robes. Ruhi watched her closely, admiring the girl's sheer confidence, even if her manners were a bit abrasive.

The girl pointed her wand directly between Harry’s eyes, aiming right at his broken spectacles. Harry tensed up automatically, leaning back against the seat.

"*Oculus Reparo*," she incanted clearly.

Instantly, a soft magical spark leaped from her wand. The thick, ugly gray tape wrapping the noseband of Harry's glasses vanished into thin air, and the plastic seamlessly mended itself, looking as good as the day it was made. Harry blinked, completely amazed, and took his glasses off to inspect them in utter disbelief.

"That's better, isn't it?" the girl said with a satisfied nod. Then, her eyes drifted up to his exposed forehead, catching sight of the jagged lightning bolt. Her breath hitched. "Holy cricket, you're Harry Potter! I'm Hermione Granger." She then snapped her head around, her sharp eyes landing on Ruhi’s elegant posture and her distinctive golden-accented clothing. "And you are...?"

Ruhi offered a polite, measured smile, her voice carrying an innate, ancient poise. "I am Ruhi. I belong from India."

Hermione’s eyes lit up with intellectual curiosity. "India! Oh, I've read all about the mystical expansion of Eastern magic in *A History of Magic*! That’s absolutely fascinating." She then finally turned her gaze toward Ron, who was currently staring at her with a half-eaten pasty stuffed in his mouth.

Ron swallowed hard, clearing his throat. "I'm Ron Weasley."

"Pleasure," Hermione said, though her tone suggested otherwise as she looked at his messy eating habits. She quickly stood up, smoothing down her robes. "You three had better change into your robes. I expect we'll be arriving soon."

She stepped out into the corridor and began to slide the door shut, but suddenly stopped. She popped her head back into the compartment, her eyes locking onto Ron with a critical squint. She pointed a finger directly at her own nose.

"You've got dirt on your nose, by the way. Did you know? Just there."

Ron’s eyes went wide with embarrassment. He instantly clapped his hand over his face, frantically scratching at the dirt mark on his nose as his face burned bright red. Hermione gave a sharp, triumphant nod and turned on her heel, her bushy hair swinging behind her as she disappeared down the corridor.

Harry and Ruhi looked at Ron’s bright red face, and then at each other. A shared, silent amusement passed between them, the mystery of Hogwarts growing deeper and more thrilling by the second.
