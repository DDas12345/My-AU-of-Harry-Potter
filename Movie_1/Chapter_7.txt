The tension on the grand stone staircase was already palpable, but it thickened the moment a young boy with slicked-back, platinum-blonde hair stepped forward from the crowd. He possessed a sharp, pale face and an air of aristocratic arrogance that immediately set him apart from the other anxious first-years.

"It's true, then, what they're saying on the train," Draco Malfoy announced, his voice carrying a slow, deliberate drawl that echoed against the high stone walls. "Harry Potter has come to Hogwarts."

Instantly, a wave of excited whispers broke out among the gathered students. *“Harry Potter?” “Did he say Harry Potter?”*

Draco took another step down the stairs, flanked by two large, thuggish-looking boys who seemed to act as his personal bodyguards. "This is Crabbe and Goyle," Draco said casually, barely gesturing to them with a flick of his wrist. "And I'm Malfoy. Draco Malfoy."

Standing right next to Harry, Ron let out a sudden, involuntary snicker, trying to muffle his laugh behind his hand.

Draco’s eyes snapped to Ron, his pale face instantly twisting into a harsh glare. "Think my name's funny, do you? I do not need to ask yours. Red hair, and a hand-me-down robe? You must be a Weasley."

Ron lowered his head, his face burning red under the sting of the insult. Draco turned back to Harry, his tone shifting into something smooth and manipulative. "You'll soon find out that some wizarding families are better than others, Potter. You don't want to go making friends with the wrong sort. I can help you there." He extended his hand toward Harry, expecting a grateful handshake.

Harry looked down at Draco's hand, his expression cold and entirely unimpressed. Remembering how kindly the Weasleys had treated him at the station, he looked straight into Draco's eyes. "I think I can tell the wrong sort for myself, thanks."

Draco’s hand remained frozen in the air, his jaw tightening as a dark flush of anger crept up his neck.

Seeking to salvage his pride and assert his dominance, Draco's gaze shifted away from Harry, scanning the nearby students until his eyes locked onto Ruhi. The young heiress of Naaglok stood with an unyielding, regal composure that radiated authority. Draco had never seen anyone like her. He stepped closer to her, his eyes narrowing with an intense, overbearing scrutiny, trying to intimidate her and force her to look away.

Ruhi stood her ground, her golden-brown eyes locked onto his. She felt an immediate wave of revulsion. This boy carried the same toxic aura of entitlement, cruelty, and arrogance that she associated with the dark forces that had torn her family apart.

As Draco continued to step into her personal space, staring at her with cold, calculating intensity, Ruhi’s ancient Naagin instincts flared to life. She refused to be objectified or intimidated by a bully.

Suddenly, a brilliant, dangerous golden scale shimmered heavily along her jawline and collarbone, catching the flickering torchlight. Her eyes underwent a terrifying transformation, bleeding from a warm brown into a piercing, luminous, supernatural gold. Before Draco could even process what he was seeing, Ruhi parted her lips slightly, and her tongue flickered out twice—thin, sharp, and distinctly split like a serpent's.

In the lore of the Ichchhaari Naagins, even in human form, the flickering of the serpent tongue was a sacred, primal defense mechanism. It was used to terrify evil, ward off saboteurs, or signal the ultimate threat of lethal venom without undergoing a full transformation.

Standing right beside her, Harry, Ron, and Hermione caught the entire display. Harry gasped softly, his hand flying to his chest in sheer shock. Ron's eyes nearly popped out of his skull, his mouth hanging completely open, while Hermione froze, her brilliant mind completely struggling to find a logical, textbook explanation for the golden scales and the reptilian tongue.

Draco stumbled back half a step, a sudden, involuntary mask of fear flashing across his pale features. But as he stared at her, the fear didn't make him back down. Instead, a twisted, dark fascination took root inside him. Realizing that there was something deeply supernatural, ancient, and immensely powerful hidden within this foreign girl, his desire to conquer and possess her influence doubled. He wasn't afraid anymore; he was captivated by the dangerous mystique she possessed. He offered her a sharp, arrogant smirk, as if accepting a challenge.

Before the confrontation could escalate any further, a sharp *tap-tap* echoed through the corridor. Professor McGonagall had returned, and she firmly tapped Draco on the shoulder with a rolled-up parchment scroll.

Draco jumped slightly, snapping out of his trance. He cast one final, lingering, intense glare at Harry and an incredibly pointed, calculating look at Ruhi before stepping back into the safety of the crowd beside Crabbe and Goyle.

Professor McGonagall stepped to the center of the grand staircase, her severe eyes lingering on Ruhi for a brief, knowing second, ensuring the young heiress was alright, before she addressed the entire group.

"We're ready for you now," McGonagall announced, her voice echoing authoritatively. "Follow me."

The massive oak doors slowly creaked open, revealing the grand, glowing expanse of the Great Hall. As the students began to march forward, Harry and Ron looked at Ruhi with a mixture of profound awe and lingering shock, realizing that their new friend carried a power far greater and more ancient than anything they had ever encountered in the wizarding world.
