The ink-black velvet of the night sky hung heavily over Hogsmeade Station as the Hogwarts Express slowed to a final, shuddering halt. The sharp, echoing scream of the locomotive’s steam whistle pierced the frosty air, sending a great plume of white vapor billowing into the darkness. The carriage doors flew open, and a vibrant sea of eleven-year-olds, all clad in their brand-new, charcoal-black school robes, began pouring out onto the chilly, dimly lit wooden platform.

Moving through the throng of shivering students, a colossal figure loomed over the crowd, instantly drawing every eye. The man was easily twice as tall as an average human, standing at a staggering eleven feet, six inches, and was at least three times as wide. He possessed a wild, magnificent mane of shaggy black hair and a tangled beard that covered the majority of his weathered face. His hands, gripping a glowing brass lantern, were easily the size of dustbin lids, and his heavy leather boots looked like pair of baby dolphins resting on the wooden planks. Beneath his bushy brows, a pair of dark eyes glinted warmly, like shiny black beetles.

"Right, then! First years! This way, please!" Hagrid’s booming voice rolled over the platform like gentle thunder. "Come on, now, first years, don't be shy! Come on now, hurry up!"

Ruhi, Harry, and Ron stepped off the train together, adjusting the heavy wool of their cloaks against the biting Scottish wind. Ruhi walked with an effortless, fluid grace, her posture completely unfazed by the sudden drop in temperature. Hidden completely beneath the voluminous folds of her black robes, the mini-dragon Agni clung tightly to her shoulder, safely concealed from public view by the ancient, seamless cloaking veil of Naaglok.

As the trio pushed through the crowd, they came a halt directly in front of the giant man.

Hagrid looked down, his rugged face instantly splitting into a massive, crinkly-eyed smile. "Hello, Harry."

"Hey, Hagrid," Harry replied, his green eyes shining with comfort at the sight of his very first friend.

Ron, standing just behind Harry, let out a soft, wheezing gasp, his eyes bulging as he craned his neck backward just to see the giant's face. "Whoa!"

But the moment Hagrid’s beetle-black eyes shifted from Harry to land squarely upon Ruhi, his cheerful expression froze. A visible tremor passed through his colossal frame. He stared at the young Indian girl—at the unmistakable, aristocratic high cheekbones, the deep, ancient intensity in her golden-brown eyes, and the subtle, regal aura that mirrored the late, great Nageshwari. Hagrid knew exactly who she was.

Deep within his mind, memories of a dark, tragic past stirred. Hagrid, alongside Albus Dumbledore and Minerva McGonagall, had been devastated by the sudden, horrifying disappearance and death of Nageshwari, a woman who had given so much to protect their school. They knew the truth: Nageshwari’s bloodline had survived. They knew her daughter, the true Heiress of Naaglok, would eventually come seeking answers. In absolute secrecy, Dumbledore had formed a hidden alliance, vowing that when the young Heiress finally arrived at Hogwarts to uncover the mysteries of her mother's murder, the inner circle of the school would act as her guardians, her informants, and her allies in the shadows.

Hagrid’s giant chest swelled with a mixture of profound sorrow and fierce protectiveness. He gave Ruhi a very subtle, respectful nod—a silent, sacred acknowledgment of their secret pact.

Ruhi caught the gesture, her sharp eyes softening with gratitude. She knew she wasn't alone in this castle; the keepers of Hogwarts were ready to help her bleed the truth from the dark.

Snapping out of his heavy thoughts, Hagrid cleared his throat roughly, swinging his lantern to break the silence. "Right then. This way to the boats! Come on, now, follow me."

---

A fleet of small, wooden rowboats sat waiting at the edge of a vast, black lake, its surface as smooth and dark as polished obsidian. The students piled in, four to a boat. As the magical vessels began to glide forward without oars, plugging effortlessly across the glassy water, the black mist parted.

Towering high above upon a colossal cliffside, its countless windows glowing like brilliant amber jewels against the dark sky, sat a magnificent, sprawling fortress of stone and magic. It was Hogwarts Castle. Turrets, spires, and high battlements reached into the heavens, casting a breathtaking, majestic reflection across the dark lake.

The first years collectively gasped, completely spellbound by the sheer grandeur of the sight.

Ron leaned over the edge of the boat, his face illuminated by the castle's distant glow. "Wicked," he whispered in absolute reverence.

Harry stared up, his heart beating with a profound sense of awe and belonging. Beside him, Ruhi looked at the massive stone fortress. To the others, it was a school of magic. To her, it was a labyrinth of ancient secrets, the very place where her father had lost his life to a dark power, and the key to avenging her mother.

---

After docking, the long procession of first-year students climbed a flight of massive stone steps, their footsteps echoing against the towering walls of the castle's entrance hall. They marched up a grand staircase, the air thick with the scent of burning torches and centuries of old magic.

Waiting at the very top of the stairs, standing before a pair of colossal oak doors, was a tall, severe-looking witch. Her emerald-green robes flowed elegantly around her, and her dark hair was gathered into a tight, neat bun beneath a pointed hat. She wore square spectacles, and her expression was one of sharp discipline. As the children approached, she tapped her fingers rhythmically against the stone railing before stepping forward to face the newcomers, her sharp eyes scanning the crowd.

"Welcome to Hogwarts," Professor McGonagall announced, her voice crisp, clear, and commanding instant silence. "Now, in a few moments, you will pass through these doors and join your classmates. But before you can take your seats, you must be sorted into your houses. They are Gryffindor, Hufflepuff, Ravenclaw, and Slytherin."

She paused, letting her stern gaze drift across the anxious faces of the children. "Now, while you're here, your house will be like your family. Your triumphs will earn you points. Any rule-breaking, and you will lose points. At the end of the year, the house with the most points is awarded the house cup."

Suddenly, a small, terrified-looking boy with a round face spotted something resting on the stone floor right near Professor McGonagall’s leather boots. His eyes lit up with frantic relief. Completely breaking protocol, he lunged forward, throwing himself to the floor.

"Trevor!" Neville Longbottom cried out, snatching up his lost toad into his hands.

The sudden outburst caused a few students to snicker and chuckle softly. Professor McGonagall stopped speaking instantly. She froze, slowly tilting her head downward, staring down her nose through her square spectacles at the trembling boy at her feet.

Neville looked up, realized what he had just done, and gulped audibly. "Sorry," he muttered sheepishly, clutching the toad tightly to his chest as he scrambled backward into the safety of the crowd, his face burning red.

McGonagall adjusted her robes, her severe expression returning. "The sorting ceremony will begin momentarily."

She turned on her heel, her green robes swishing gracefully as she prepared to walk through the heavy oak doors into the Great Hall. But just before she pushed the doors open, her sharp eyes swept across the crowd one last time—and stopped directly on Ruhi.

McGonagall’s breath caught in her throat. For a fraction of a second, the stern, unyielding professor looked utterly, profoundly shocked. In Ruhi’s regal posture, the deliberate, fearless calm of her stance, McGonagall saw the living image of Nageshwari. The weight of their secret alliance pressed heavily into her mind. The Heiress had finally arrived.

Masking her shock with practiced, professional ease, McGonagall turned and slipped through the doors, leaving the students waiting in a buzz of anticipation.

Harry and Ron looked toward the doors, but Ruhi kept her gaze fixed on the spot where McGonagall had stood, a cold, sharp determination settling into her ancient Naagin soul. The pieces were moving. The alliance was active.
