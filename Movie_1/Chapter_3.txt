The echo of the twins vanishing through the solid brick barrier still lingered in the air as Harry and Ruhi exchanged a look of shared determination. There was no turning back now. Ruhi gripped the handle of her luggage trolley, her knuckles whitening slightly, while beside her, Harry gave his own trolley a tentative, nervous push forward.

Together, the two eleven-year-olds approached the plump, red-haired woman who was now adjusting the coat of her youngest son.

"Excuse me," Harry spoke up, his voice a little small against the roaring din of King's Cross Station.

The woman turned around, her warm, motherly face instantly lighting up with a kind, welcoming smile as she looked at the two lost-looking children. "Excuse me, big sister," Ruhi added, her English clear and flawlessly polite, using a respectful manner of address that came naturally to her, even in a foreign land. "Could you perhaps tell us how to...?"

"How to get onto the platform?" Molly Weasley finished gently, her eyes crinkling with instant understanding. She looked at Harry’s messy hair and then at Ruhi’s elegant, distinct features and the unusual silk-covered cage on her cart. "Not to worry, dears. It's Ron's first time at Hogwarts as well."

She motioned with her hand toward the youngest red-haired boy standing beside her. The boy, Ron, was tall and gangly, with freckles dusting his nose and a smudge of dirt near his ear. Sensing the attention on him, Ron offered Harry and Ruhi a nervous but genuinely friendly smile, giving a small, awkward wave of his hand.

Molly turned her attention back to the two anxious newcomers, her voice lowering into a reassuring, conspiratorial tone. "Now, all you've got to do is walk straight at the wall between platforms 9 and 10. Best do it at a bit of a run if you're nervous."

Ruhi looked at the solid, immovable red brick pillar. To a normal human eye, it was an absolute guarantee of a concussion. But Ruhi closed her eyes for a brief second, tapping into her inner sight. She didn't use her full *dibya drishti*, but she let her Naagin senses feel the air currents. Beneath the smell of coal and diesel, she detected a heavy, ancient ripple of magic woven directly into the mortar of the wall—a magical veil meant to pass through.

Beside them, the small red-haired girl, Ginny, looked up at Harry and then at Ruhi with wide, admire-filled eyes. She took a step forward, her voice full of genuine warmth. "Good luck, both of you."

"Thank you," Ruhi replied with a serene, confident nod.

Harry took a deep, shuddering breath, aligning his trolley perfectly with the center of the barrier. "Together?" he whispered to Ruhi, looking for a bit of solidarity.

"Together," Ruhi replied, a sharp, fearless glint entering her golden-brown eyes.

Inside his cage, Agni let out a low, muffled hiss of anticipation, sensing the shift in his mistress's energy. Ruhi tightened her grip on the metal bar of her trolley. Harry braced his feet against the concrete.

"Three... two... one... *go!*" Harry muttered.

They broke into a sudden sprint, pushing their heavy carts ahead of them. The brick wall rushed toward them at terrifying speed. Harry automatically leaned his head down, closing his eyes tightly, bracing himself for the violent impact of metal and bone shattering against stone. Ruhi kept her eyes wide open, her fierce royal gaze locked onto the target, refusing to flinch.

The distance closed—three feet, two feet, one foot—

*Whoosh.*

There was no crash. Instead, a sudden, blinding flash of cold, misty air washed over them. The heavy scent of London diesel evaporated instantly, replaced by the rich, thick smell of burning coal, old iron, and something intensely magical. The violent clatter of their trolley wheels on concrete transitioned into a smooth, echoing roll over a dark, cobblestone floor.

Harry snapped his eyes open, slowing his run into a halt. Ruhi gracefully brought her trolley to a stop right beside him, her hair shifting softly with the sudden change in atmospheric pressure.

They looked up, and their breath was collectively stolen away.

They were standing on a magnificent, hidden train platform bathed in a warm, ethereal light that seemed to filter down from a massive glass arched roof high above. Towering over the tracks was a glorious, gleaming scarlet steam locomotive, thick white clouds of steam billowing lazily from its funnel. A large, elegant gold plate on the front read: **The Hogwarts Express.**

The platform was an absolute kaleidoscope of magical chaos. Bundles of people in flowing robes of every color imaginable were rushing back and forth. Owls of all shapes and sizes hooted from atop mountains of trunks; cats darted between the legs of busy parents; and old friends were loudly reuniting.

Harry looked around in absolute, spellbound wonder, a massive, brilliant smile breaking across his face. He looked over at Ruhi.

Ruhi, usually so guarded and stoic from her years of rigorous warrior training in the Shiva Mandir, couldn't help the pure excitement bubbling up in her chest. She looked at the scarlet train—the gateway to the man who had stolen her mother’s life. The hunt was officially beginning, but the sheer grandeur of the wizarding world was undeniable.

As the engine's loud, echoing steam whistle blew—a sharp *HOOT* that reverberated through the very bones of the station—Harry and Ruhi looked at each other, and they both grinned from ear to ear.
