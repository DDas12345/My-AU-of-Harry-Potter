The afternoon sun poured through the high, arched windows of the Great Hall, illuminating dust motes that danced above the long wooden tables. Midday had arrived, and the hall was filled with the relaxed chatter of Gryffindor students catching up on homework, flipping through heavy parchment books, or practicing simple charms.

Sitting together near the middle of the table, Harry, Ron, Hermione, and Ruhi were huddled over their respective parchment rolls. A few seats down, Seamus Finnigan was staring intently into a goblet of plain water, his wand tapped against the rim.

"Eye of rabbit, harp string hum," Seamus chanted, swirling his wand with hopeful concentration. "Turn this water into rum."

He leaned over, peeking into the glass, only to see the water sloshing unchanged. He shook his head in disappointment, took a deep breath, and tried again.

"Eye of rabbit, harp string hum..."

Harry paused his quill, looking up in amusement. "What’s Seamus trying to do to that glass of water?"

Ron leaned over his inkwell, smirking. "Turn it into rum. He actually managed to make weak tea yesterday, before..."

*BANG!*

A blinding flash of yellow light cut Ron off as the goblet violently exploded into a cloud of thick, black smoke. When the haze cleared, Seamus was left sitting upright with his hair standing on end, his face covered in dark soot, and his eyebrows thoroughly singed.

Several Gryffindor students burst into loud laughter. Hermione sighed, waving her hand frantically to disperse the lingering smoke from their parchment, while Ruhi let out a quiet, graceful chuckle, shaking her head softly at Seamus's explosive attempt.

Before Seamus could clean his face, a sudden rush of cool wind swept through the rafters high above. The grand hall echoed with the soft rustle of hundreds of wings.

Ron’s face lit up as he looked toward the high ceiling. "Ah. Mail’s here!"

A massive flock of owls swooped gracefully into the Great Hall, carrying letters, rolled newspapers, and brown-paper parcels in their talons, dropping them down onto the long house tables.

Harry watched longingly as owls landed near other students, but none stopped in front of him. His eyes fell upon a copy of the *Daily Prophet* that Ron had unrolled beside his plate.

"Can I borrow this?" Harry asked, pointing to the newspaper.

Ron nodded around a mouthful of toast. "Thanks," Harry muttered, pulling the paper closer.

A few seats over, Neville Longbottom was excitedly untying the string of a small, square package. He pulled away the brown paper, revealing a delicate, clear glass ball wrapped in a golden band.

Dean Thomas leaned in, pointing. "Hey, look! Neville’s got a Remembrall!"

Hermione looked up from her book, her sharp eyes instantly identifying the magical object. "I’ve read about those. When the smoke turns red..."

As if on cue, a thick swirl of crimson smoke filled the interior of the glass sphere.

"...it means you’ve forgotten something," Hermione finished.

Neville stared blankly at the glowing red ball in his palm, his face twisting into complete confusion. "The only problem is, I can’t remember what I’ve forgotten."

While Neville stared at his hand, Harry was scanning the front page of the *Daily Prophet*. His eyes widened as a specific headline caught his attention. He quickly tapped Ron's arm.

"Hey, Ron! Somebody broke into Gringotts. Listen." Harry cleared his throat and read aloud: "‘Believed to be the work of dark witches or wizards unknown, Gringotts goblins, while acknowledging the breach, insist that nothing was taken. The vault in question, number 713, had in fact been emptied earlier that same day.’"

Harry looked up from the paper, his green eyes dark with suspicion. "That’s odd. That’s the vault Hagrid and I went to."

Beside him, Hermione narrowed her eyes in deep thought, exchanging a sharp glance with Harry. Sitting across from them, Ruhi’s golden-brown eyes darkened with an intense, quiet focus. Her Naagin instincts instantly connected the dots—Vault 713 had contained something so vital that both Dumbledore and a mysterious dark force were pursuing it.

Before anyone could speak further, a majestic, ethereal shadow glided across their table, blocking the sunlight.

Harry, Ron, Hermione, Ruhi, and every student sitting nearby looked up in absolute awe. Soaring gracefully beneath the enchanted ceiling was a magnificent, royal Indian Eagle Owl. Its striking yellow eyes shone like amber, and its deep brown and golden-feathered wings spanned remarkably wide.

In its talons, the owl carried a velvet-wrapped parcel bound with sacred red and gold silk threads. The bird swooped down smoothly, landing with quiet, majestic precision right in front of Ruhi. It bowed its head slightly, presenting the parcel to her as if delivering a sacred, royal tribute.

Ruhi’s heart skipped a beat. She stared at the magnificent bird in utter astonishment. "I was not expecting any parcel..." she whispered softly.

With gentle hands, Ruhi stroked the owl’s golden feathers in gratitude before carefully untying the silk threads. As she unpeeled the soft velvet cloth, a subtle, radiant warmth radiated across the Gryffindor table.

Resting inside was a pristine, translucent Shiva Linga, exquisitely hand-carved from a single block of pure, radiant crystal. It possessed a magnificent, solid weight—a heavy, sacred gift that glittered under the Great Hall’s floating candles, casting dazzling rainbow reflections across the wooden table.

A collective gasp of sheer wonder rippled through the Gryffindor table.

"Blimey..." Ron breathed, leaning in close, his eyes wide. "That's unbelievable."

"It's breathtaking," Hermione whispered, completely mesmerized by the flawless clarity of the crystal.

Ruhi uncovered a small piece of rolled parchment tucked beneath the velvet. The moment her eyes fell upon the delicate, familiar Hindi script, an emotional, tender smile broke across her face. The letter was from her beloved Gurumaa, Uttara Maa. Though Uttara was her master and elder in Naaglok, she had raised Ruhi with the unconditional, fierce love of a *naatin*—treating her as her very own beloved granddaughter. The letter contained blessings of protection, reminding Ruhi that Mahadev’s divine light would always guide her through the darkness of Hogwarts.

Sitting a few seats down, Parvati Patil—a Gryffindor girl who shared Ruhi's Indian heritage—stared at the radiant crystal object. Recognizing the sacred form instantly, Parvati’s eyes lit up with profound reverence.

Without hesitation, Parvati stepped closer to Ruhi's seat, folded her hands together in a respectful *Namaste*, and joined her palms before the divine crystal Shiva Linga.

Seeing Parvati's gesture, Ruhi's smile softened with deep spiritual warmth. Ruhi leaned forward, closing her eyes in utter devotion, and gently touched her forehead to the cool, luminous crystal Shiva Linga, paying her silent homage to Lord Shiva right there in the middle of the Great Hall.

Harry watched Ruhi with quiet, profound admiration. The deep devotion, regal grace, and ancient purity she radiated touched his heart, weaving an even stronger, unspoken bond between them amidst the growing mysteries of Hogwarts.
