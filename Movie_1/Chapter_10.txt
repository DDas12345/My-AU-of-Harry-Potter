The cool autumn sunlight filtered through the lofty, arched stained-glass windows of Hogwarts Castle, illuminating the vast stone corridors. High above, echoing footsteps and chiming tower bells signaled the imminent start of morning lessons.

In a mad dash down the wide, moving staircases, Harry and Ron were sprinting for their lives. Their robes billowed wildly behind them, and their parchment scrolls rattled inside their leather satchels as their shoes slapped heavily against the cobblestone floors.

"Which way, Harry? Left or right?!" Ron panted, his face bright red as he gripped his sliding satchel strap.

"I think it's straight down this corridor!" Harry called out, checking his wrist before picking up the pace.

Inside the quiet Transfiguration classroom, the air was thick with the scent of old parchment, chalk, and polished wood. Up at the front, perched with rigid, unnatural posture atop the teacher's heavy oak desk, sat a dark tabby cat. The feline wore distinctive square markings around its eyes, precisely mirroring a pair of spectacles, and its tail flicked with silent, absolute authority.

Near the front row, Hermione Granger sat with her textbook *A Beginner's Guide to Transfiguration* open, her quill neatly aligned. Sitting directly beside her was Ruhi. The young heiress of Naaglok looked effortless and immaculate, her black school cloak tailored gracefully over her uniform, her dark hair sweeping over her shoulders.

Suddenly, the heavy oak doors burst open with a loud *BANG!*

Harry and Ron stumbled into the room, leaning against a stone archway, completely out of breath.

Hermione slowly rolled her eyes, letting out a small, disappointed sigh at their utter lack of punctuality. Ruhi, however, turned in her seat and offered the two boys a soft, reassuring smile—her warm golden-brown eyes fixing on Harry with a gentle spark. She was genuinely relieved that they had at least managed to find the room before the lesson was officially called to order.

Yet, as Ruhi glanced back toward the desk, her sharp, ancient Naagin instincts flared. Her gaze locked onto the quiet tabby cat. Being an Ichchhaari Naagin herself—capable of shifting into any human, serpent, or supernatural form at will—Ruhi instantly recognized the immense, dense magical aura surrounding the animal. She knew with absolute certainty that this was no ordinary feline; it was Professor McGonagall in her Animagus form. A subtle flicker of concern passed through Ruhi's eyes as she watched Ron stumble right into the trap.

Ron doubled over, resting his hands on his knees, letting out a massive breath of relief. "Whew! Made it! Can you imagine McGonagall's face if we were late?"

The words had barely left his lips when the tabby cat gave a sudden, fluid leap right off the desk!

In mid-air, the cat's form expanded, stretched, and shifted seamlessly into the tall, slender figure of Professor McGonagall. Her emerald-green robes swished as her leather boots touched the stone floor, her stern face settling into a sharp, unyielding look as she adjusted her spectacles.

Harry’s jaw dropped. Ron froze, staring at her in absolute, wide-eyed amazement.

"That was bloody brilliant!" Ron blurted out before he could stop himself.

Professor McGonagall stepped forward, her hands folded tightly in front of her. "Thank you for that assessment, Mr. Weasley. Perhaps it would be more useful if I transfigured you and Mr. Potter into a pocketwatch. Then at least one of you might be on time."

Harry cleared his throat nervously, trying to defend them. "We got lost."

"Then perhaps a map?" McGonagall countered instantly, her sharp brow rising. "I trust you don't need one to find your seats."

Without another word, Harry and Ron scurried down the aisle, pulling out two empty chairs directly behind Ruhi and Hermione. They dropped their heavy bags onto the floor, sitting down with hushed, embarrassed haste.

Ruhi turned around gracefully in her chair, leaning back slightly so only the two boys could hear her soft, melodic voice.

"You both run fast, but this castle's staircases love to play tricks on those who hurry," Ruhi whispered, her golden-brown eyes shining with a mixture of warm humor and genuine care. She glanced over at Hermione, who gave a firm, approving nod, before turning back to Harry and Ron. "From now on, all four of us—Hermione, Harry, Ron, and I—will walk to our classes together as a team. That way, no one gets lost, and no one is ever late again."

Harry looked into Ruhi’s reassuring eyes, feeling a sudden, comforting warmth bloom in his chest. A small, grateful smile broke across his face. "Thanks, Ruhi. That sounds like a really good plan."

Ron slouched in his seat, rubbing his neck sheepishly, but offered a thankful nod. "Yeah... definitely better than being turned into a pocket watch."

Hermione turned around as well, a satisfied smirk on her face. "I'll make sure our morning departure schedule is mathematically precise."

Ruhi let out a quiet, graceful chuckle before turning back to face the front, her heart light. The four of them were officially forming an unbreakable bond, and with Ruhi's protective, sharp instincts guiding them, they were ready to take on whatever challenges the castle had in store.
