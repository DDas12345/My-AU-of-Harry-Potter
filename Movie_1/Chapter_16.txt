The crisp autumn wind swept across the expansive green lawns of Hogwarts, rustling the golden leaves along the perimeter of the majestic Quidditch pitch in the distance. The towering spectator stands topped with colorful house banners stood silent and empty against the bright blue afternoon sky.

Oliver Wood, the Gryffindor Quidditch Captain, strode out onto the open courtyard carrying a heavy, brass-bound wooden trunk. Trailing right beside him were Harry and Ruhi, both dressed in their comfortable casual clothes, their eyes filled with eager anticipation.

Oliver set the heavy trunk down onto the stone ground with a dull *thud*, popping open the metal clasps to reveal three distinct compartments inside.

"Quidditch is easy enough to understand," Oliver began, pacing back and forth with the fiery enthusiasm of a veteran captain. "Each team has seven players. Three Chasers, two Beaters, one Keeper, and a Seeker. That's you, Harry. And Ruhi, you're one of our three Chasers alongside Alicia Spinnet and Katie Bell."

Oliver reached into the center of the trunk and picked up a smooth, leather ball roughly the size of a soccer ball, painted a bright crimson red.

"There are three kinds of balls," Oliver explained, holding up the red ball for both of them to see. "This one's called the Quaffle. Now, the Chasers handle the Quaffle and try to put it through one of those three hoops." He pointed across the grounds toward the tall golden goal posts rising high above the pitch walls. "The Keeper—that's me—defends the hoops."

Oliver threw the Quaffle smoothly toward Harry. Harry caught it cleanly in both hands, feeling its weight before throwing it right back to Oliver.

"With me so far?" Oliver asked, catching the ball.

"I think so," Harry replied with a confident nod.

Ruhi stepped forward, her dark hair catching the breeze, her golden-brown eyes fixed on the remaining contents of the trunk. "And the scoring system, Oliver? How many points does a goal yield?"

"Ten points every time a Chaser gets the Quaffle through one of the opposing hoops," Oliver answered cleanly, offering Ruhi a nod of approval. He set the Quaffle back in its leather groove, then turned his attention to two violent, heavy iron spheres that were squirming frantically against heavy leather straps.

Harry pointed at the struggling iron balls. "What are those?"

"You better take this," Oliver said, reaching into the side of the trunk and handing Harry a small, heavy wooden bat.

Oliver bent down carefully, unbuckling the leather strap of one of the iron spheres. The moment the latch freed, the ball let out a low, angry metallic growl and shot straight up into the sky like a cannonball!

Harry and Ruhi shielded their eyes, watching the heavy ball zoom high above the courtyard archways.

"Careful now, it's coming back!" Oliver warned, tracking its trajectory.

The ball turned sharply in mid-air, whizzing straight down toward the trio with terrifying speed. Harry gripped the short bat with both hands, took a step back, and cracked at the ball with a powerful swing! *CLANG!* The iron ball soared off toward a stone statue of a medieval knight, passing cleanly through the archway above it without touching a stone.

Oliver watched the flight with wide eyes, genuinely impressed. "Not bad, Potter! You'd make a fair Beater! Uh-oh..."

The ball veered wildly around the stone tower and zoomed straight back down toward their heads. Oliver dropped to his knees, throwing his arms out and wrestling the thrashing iron ball back into the trunk. He buckled the leather strap tight, panting heavily as he wiped a bead of sweat from his forehead.

Harry stared at the locked trunk, his jaw slightly slack. "What was that?!"

"Bludger," Oliver wheezed out, catching his breath. "Nasty little buggers. Their job is to knock players off their brooms. That's why Fred and George stay busy." He stood up, turning his full attention back to Harry. "But you are a Seeker. And the only thing I want you to worry about is this."

Oliver reached into a tiny, velvet-lined slot at the center of the trunk and carefully lifted out a smooth, polished golden sphere no larger than a walnut. He handed it gently to Harry.

"The Golden Snitch," Oliver introduced softly.

Harry held the tiny golden ball in his palm, examining its polished surface. "I like this ball."

Oliver chuckled grimly. "Ah, you like it now. Just wait. It's wicked fast and damn near impossible to see."

"What do I do with it?" Harry asked, looking up.

"You catch it," Oliver stated plainly. "Before the other team's Seeker. You catch this, the game is over. You catch this, Potter, and we win. It earns the team a massive one hundred and fifty points."

Suddenly, two delicate, paper-thin silver wings sprouted from the sides of the golden walnut. The Snitch hovered in place for a microsecond before jumping straight into the air with a soft buzzing sound. Harry's eyes tracked its lightning-fast movement across the courtyard.

"Whoa!" Harry breathed, mesmerized.

While Harry watched the Snitch flutter high above, Oliver turned around and picked the red Quaffle back up from the trunk. He tossed it across to Ruhi.

"Now, Ruhi," Oliver said, his voice sharpening with intense tactical focus. "As a Chaser, your job requires continuous movement, rapid passing, and absolute precision. You'll be flying in formation with the other two Chasers, dodging Bludgers, and breaking through the opposing team's defensive line."

Ruhi caught the leather Quaffle effortlessly with one hand, spinning it gracefully on her fingertips. Her Ichhadhaari Naagin instincts—honed by centuries of sacred martial agility and spatial awareness—instantly evaluated the weight, air resistance, and center of gravity of the ball.

"Precision under pressure is second nature to me, Oliver," Ruhi spoke softly, her voice cool and steady. "Show me the target."

Oliver smiled, gesturing toward the far end of the courtyard where three wooden training hoops had been set up on iron stands, roughly thirty yards away. "Let's see your throwing arm from the ground first. Most first-years struggle with the weight of the Quaffle at a distance."

Ruhi didn't even pause to take a running start. With fluid, effortless elegance, she stepped forward, her arm whipping back in a motion as smooth as a striking cobra. She launched the Quaffle into the air!

The ball cut through the afternoon wind in a perfect, razor-sharp arc, whistling past the stone pillars and sailing dead-center right through the smallest top hoop with a crisp, echoing *SWISH!*

The Quaffle bounced onto the grass on the other side, rolling to a gentle stop.

Oliver’s mouth hung open in absolute, stunned silence. He blinked once, then twice, looking from the distant hoop back to Ruhi.

"Merlin's beard..." Oliver gasped out, running a hand through his hair. "That was... thirty yards out! Without even being on a broom! The trajectory was completely flawless!"

Harry turned away from watching the Snitch, his face lighting up with immense pride and delight as he looked at Ruhi. "That was incredible, Ruhi! You hit it right in the center!"

Ruhi offered a serene, modest smile, her golden-brown eyes sparkling under the sunlight as she met Harry's warm gaze. "A warrior must always know the path to the mark before the strike is delivered, Harry. In the air, with you spotting the Snitch and me breaking their lines, Gryffindor will be unstoppable."

Oliver clapped his hands together with wild excitement, a huge grin spreading across his face. "Unstoppable doesn't even cover it! Between Potter's natural flight instincts and your deadly aim, Slytherin won't even know what hit them when match day comes!"

As the golden Snitch buzzed lazily above their heads and the cool Scottish autumn breeze swept across the lawn, Harry and Ruhi shared a quiet, confident smile—ready to take on the skies of Hogwarts together.
